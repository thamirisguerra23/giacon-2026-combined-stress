# Physiological responses of industrial *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* to combined stress from lignocellulosic inhibitors and lactic acid bacteria

Data and analysis code for the manuscript:

> Giacon, T. G., Vilela, N., Olsson, L., Basso, T. O. Physiological responses of industrial *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* to combined stress from lignocellulosic inhibitors and lactic acid bacteria. [Journal of Industrial Biotechnology], [2026]. DOI: [to be added]

## Overview

The industrial yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* PE-2 was cultivated alone or in co-culture with
*Limosilactobacillus fermentum* Ia3, *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* Ia4, or both strains, in the
presence of nine individual lignocellulosic inhibitors (HMF, furfural, acetic, formic, glycolic,
levulinic and ferulic acids, syringaldehyde and vanillin) or without inhibitor. Yeast viability,
yeast and bacterial growth, extracellular metabolites and final pH were measured after 24 h.

## Repository structure

    .
    ├── statistic_code.Rmd    # all statistical tests and figures of the paper
    ├── statistic_code.html   # rendered report (download and open in a browser)
    ├── data/
    │   ├── viability.csv              # yeast viability (%), methylene blue staining
    │   ├── bubbleplot.xlsx            # yeast growth ratio (final/initial cell concentration)
    │   ├── bubbleplot_bacteria.xlsx   # bacterial growth ratio (final/initial CFU/mL)
    │   ├── metabolites.xlsx           # ethanol, glycerol, acetate, lactate (g/L), one sheet each
    │   ├── ph.csv                     # final pH of the cultures
    │   ├── yeast_foldchange.csv       # yeast growth ratio, long format (competition index, PCA)
    │   ├── lab_foldchange.csv         # bacterial growth ratio, long format (competition index, PCA)
    │   └── hplc_all_raw.csv           # HPLC concentrations (g/L) at 0, 2, 4, 8 and 24 h
    ├── Figures/                       # figures generated by the Rmd (TIFF, 600 dpi)
    └── Figures_pdf/                   # same figures in PDF

In the wide-format files (`viability.csv`, `ph.csv` and the `.xlsx` files), each row is one
inhibitor condition (inhibitor-free control first) and each column is one inoculation condition
and replicate: `none` (yeast only), `LF`, `LP` and `LFLP` (both strains), replicates 1 to 3.

The long-format files have one row per culture. In `hplc_all_raw.csv`, the column `arm` keeps the
letter used in the HPLC sample names, which differs from the assay letters of the manuscript:
A = *L. fermentum*, B = *L. plantarum*, C = both strains, D = yeast only. The Rmd converts these
letters automatically. Acetate values for levulinic acid are excluded from the analyses because
levulinic acid co-elutes with acetate.

## How to reproduce the analyses

1. Install R (version 4.3 or later) and the required packages:

```r
   install.packages(c("rmarkdown", "knitr", "readxl", "readr", "dplyr", "tidyr",
                      "ggplot2", "ggrepel", "patchwork", "multcomp", "scales",
                      "pheatmap", "vegan"))
```

2. Download or clone this repository and open `statistic_code.Rmd` in RStudio.
3. Click **Knit**. The report and all figures are regenerated.

## Statistical approach

Each inhibitor was tested in a separate experimental block (different day, independent inoculum,
own bacteria-free control). Comparisons are therefore made within blocks: one-way ANOVA, Dunnett's
test against the bacteria-free control and Tukey's test among contaminated assays. Growth ratios
are analysed on the log scale.

In addition to the per-variable tests, the Rmd includes:

- **Competition index** between yeast and bacteria, log2(yeast fold change) − log2(bacterial
  fold change) calculated within each culture, tested against zero (one-sample t-test,
  Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and against the inhibitor-free condition (Dunnett's test).
- **Multivariate analysis** of nine responses (yeast growth and viability, bacterial growth,
  glucose consumed after 4 h, ethanol, glycerol, lactate and acetate production, final pH),
  each expressed relative to the bacteria-free culture of the same inhibitor: principal
  component analysis, hierarchical clustering (Ward's method) and PERMANOVA (vegan, 9,999
  permutations) testing the effects of contamination type and inhibitor chemical class.

See the Rmd for details.
